# Product validation and alternatives

Updated 10 October 2026; alternatives research refreshed 10 October 2026. This document defines what would establish that Line solves a valuable problem. It contains research, hypotheses and acceptance gates; it contains **no customer interviews, pilot results, signed commitments, revenue or confirmed production payouts**. Interview counts below are recruitment targets only, not pass/fail thresholds, market-prevalence estimates or substitutes for decision-quality evidence.

## Product thesis and current status

Line is testing whether organizations need issuer-authorized agent purchasing with less sensitive credit-policy disclosure and more reliable supplier reconciliation. That is a hypothesis, not a demonstrated market. Privacy refers to private borrower headroom `H = L - B`, not public aggregate reserve availability `T - E - R - F - P - U - X`; note amounts and reserve totals/deltas are intentionally public. A linked history can reconstruct borrower debt from a known-zero opening before a private repayment, so this is not full historical confidentiality. The current checkout is local generated execution without funded payment or independent supplier custody.

The customer segment is not selected. Compare three discovery cohorts independently: (A) production agent-platform operators buying for their own workflow, (B) enterprise teams operating agents for internal API/compute procurement, including the user's initial enterprise-team hypothesis, and (C) platforms integrating for a named customer that is the actual buyer/borrower. For each cohort, seek the same five evidence items: (1) a recent agent-originated purchase or blocked attempt, amount/frequency, workflow owner and software budget owner/budget line; (2) the named people/organizations that authorized, legally bought/borrowed, paid, fulfilled and reconciled it; (3) an evidenced problem (measured manual burden, cost, delay, failure or confidentiality harm) with a baseline; (4) a transaction-specific explanation of why the strongest viable delegated wallet, x402/MPP, card, prepaid account, invoice or gateway did not solve it; and (5) the accountable borrower, capital provider and settlement/custody owner, or an explicit statement that one or more are unknown. For an integrator, include the customer buyer/borrower and establish who contracts with Line. Unknown roles are documented as gaps, not assumed. Treasury-only organizations without an operated agent workflow are a separate control cohort. Future plans and generic procurement frustration do not qualify. Record high-volume low-value calls separately from larger account-billed API/compute invoices. Select a lead segment only after observing materially different job frequency, buyer authority, liability, integration/sales channel and alternative performance across cohorts.

The falsifiable “why Line” hypothesis is: a buyer will pay for a shared facility when neither each agent's wallet nor a trusted gateway can give multiple independent suppliers an acceptable, independently verifiable clearance without revealing the buyer's wider credit book, and the same flow can settle and reconcile actual purchases at an acceptable cost. The trigger must be observed in a recent transaction, not prompted in an interview. For each candidate, run the same task against the incumbent and the strongest viable wallet/x402, Machine Payments Protocol (MPP), gateway and invoice alternative; count Line as differentiated only if the budget owner identifies a specific unmet control, privacy or financing requirement, accepts its trade-off and purchases the priced pilot. If per-call caps alone solve the job, integrate them; if suppliers trust a gateway and independent verification adds no value, prefer the simpler gateway. Such results would disconfirm the current beachhead, not waive the full product's privacy, fleet, capital, settlement and risk requirements.

The product destination remains complete private revolving credit and checkout: multiple agents, independent suppliers and capital providers, confidential books under a named observer model, authenticated funding and settlement, durable recovery, default/dispute processes and portable credit history. The earliest procurement-control pilot would not establish independent lending or full privacy. No customer interviews, signed pilot, merchant acceptance, customer contacts or external payment receipts are currently established.

There is no supported basis here for claiming that ordinary cards expose credit limits to all merchants, that competing wallets have no spending controls, or that a merchant sees an entire allowance whenever it accepts a payment. Ask where disclosure actually occurs and inspect the alternative's real API, ledger and custody boundaries.

The feature table is a screening inventory, not a competitive win/loss study. In each pilot compare the same request, amount, latency target and failure/retry cases; use the alternative's current documented version and tested payment method; record integration hours, custody/key model, proof/finality, liability, support, refunds, and all quoted/measured costs. Keep small-call batching comparisons separate from invoice-scale compute procurement. x402/MPP provide payment mechanisms; do not treat them as proof of credit underwriting or assume they provide Line's proposed shared-credit model.

## Precise differentiation and its limits

Line's proposed difference is independently verifiable issuer-authorized purchasing with a merchant-owned claim and a reconcilable purchase. This is a destination hypothesis, not a current product advantage: today public amounts and history expose substantial usage, the agent secret participates in issuer-authenticated line opening, and neither merchant payout nor service delivery is verified by Compact. Customer preference remains unvalidated.

The present generated-Compact execution establishes only that a local authenticated state transition satisfied its encoded rules; no ZK proof is submitted or verified by the current checkout. It does not establish independent role custody, honest underwriting, real-world cash reserves, useful service delivery or an external bank payment. Those require separate evidence and responsible parties.

Midnight explicitly supports public verification with private state and selective disclosure. Its runtime distinguishes witness outputs from the public operation transcript; using private witnesses therefore does not hide intentionally public arguments, queries or ledger deltas. [Midnight overview](https://docs.midnight.network/), [CallProofData API](https://docs.midnight.network/api-reference/compact-runtime/interfaces/CallProofData).

For the existing single-line/public-note design, the audit demonstrated merchant-key disclosure and historical debt inference from public note amounts before the first private repayment. A commitment may hide its opening while the transaction sequence reveals the same quantity. The target product must resolve this architectural gap before it claims historical book confidentiality. Merely suppressing fields in the explorer or removing merchant keys from a stored struct does not satisfy that requirement. Review the complete public transcript, amounts, aggregate deltas, line identity, timing and counterparty knowledge. [Audit privacy findings](FULL_AUDIT_2026-10-07.md#f05--p0-for-privacy-claims-merchant-identity-is-disclosed-in-public-execution-transcripts).

Do not claim exclusive invention of confidential credit, globally unique agent payments, guaranteed merchant anonymity, zero leakage, instant finality or superior economics from this research. Earn narrower comparisons with reproducible traces and measurements.

## Full product requirements that cannot disappear

| Requirement | Evidence required before marking it achieved |
|---|---|
| Independent roles | Separate issuer, agent and merchant processes/custody contexts; domain-bound line commitments pass cross-instance tests; existing installations have an explicit versioned migration; agent tools cannot fund reserves, open arbitrary lines or acknowledge repayments; A cannot act as B |
| Actual purchase | Agent selects and accepts authenticated terms; merchant verifies authorization, returns a useful service result, and provides an authenticated receipt bound to the request and note |
| Real economic backing | Specify the settlement asset, unit scale, custody and capital provider; demonstrate a deposit/payout or a verifiable external reconciliation; a `fundReserve` counter is insufficient |
| Revolving credit | Identify liable organization, issuer exposure, repayment source, credit policy, fee terms and collections owner; one accepted external receipt restores capacity once |
| Multiple agents | Enrollment, isolation, per-agent limits and concurrent draws work; aggregate issued credit and settled liabilities reconcile; the singleton contract is not evidence of a pooled fleet facility |
| Multiple merchants | Independent keys, identities and terms; registry disable/revocation semantics; merchant-specific delivery and redemption; real operational acceptance of the settlement terms |
| Credit-book privacy | Preserve hidden $L$, $B$ and borrower headroom `H = L - B`; explicitly disclose public note amounts/reserve deltas and aggregate reserve availability; test what each observer can infer from its full accessible history and limit claims to evidence |
| Correct prices and fees | Asset/unit/rounding rules; quote-bound or authenticated fee schedule; agent cannot remove required charges; fees are recognized as revenue only on actual collection |
| Reliable settlement | Distinguish authorized, claim issued, claim redeemed, payout pending, payout confirmed and delivered; bind asset receipts to the exact claim; prevent payout retries from paying twice |
| Failure and recovery | Durable purchase journal and current private openings survive restart; unknown confirmation is reconciled before retry; no lost commitment opening, duplicate debt, payout or unintended repeat execution |
| Refunds and disputes | Define failure after draw/before delivery, after redemption/before payout and after delivery; determine who owes money and who can resolve disputes; cancellation must not silently charge undelivered work |
| Issuer risk controls | Enforce suspension/default, exposure limits, merchant eligibility, fee policy and safe reopening; preserve valid merchant claims and historical liabilities through lifecycle changes |
| Adoption | Tested merchant SDK/API, typed agent interface and onboarding; a supplier integrates without understanding Compact internals or sharing its key with the buyer |
| Performance and operations | Measured proof time, finality, throughput, cold start, costs and recovery; capacity limits, monitoring, reconciliation and support ownership |

The twelve authorized circuits constrain implementation choices. Preserve their names and roles; map product needs to changes in supported circuit semantics, existing adapters, or separately specified off-chain services. Do not invent Compact APIs. Before declaring privacy feasibility, maintain a reviewed transition map for all twelve names: merchant enrollment/revocation (`registerMerchant`, `disableMerchant`); custody-counter updates (`fundReserve`, `withdrawUnencumberedReserve`, `withdrawFees`) plus external deposit/payment proof; issuer-authorized borrower enrollment (`openLine`) with independent agent custody; buyer-accepted terms (`postQuote`); borrower-leaf and reserve-conserving spend (`draw`); one-use merchant claim (`redeemDraw`) plus a rail payout receipt; expiry/refund allocation (`cancelOrExpireNote`); externally evidenced, globally deduplicated repayment (`acknowledgeRepayment`); and debt-preserving close/default/recovery (`setStatus`). For each row specify the public fields, private update, authority, latest-root/concurrency rule, external evidence and upgrade/migration behavior. This is an architecture-spike deliverable, not evidence of feasibility. If a required invariant cannot be implemented using the twelve names and supported Compact APIs, document the incompatibility and keep the affected product claim/release blocked. Passing old tests cannot override product requirements.

## Falsifiable customer hypotheses

Use an initial recruitment target of six organizations, aiming for two independent organizations in each buyer cohort, and three suppliers; allocate interviews to test competing hypotheses rather than satisfy a quota. Two cases per cohort are only a minimum comparison floor, not proof of repetition or a statistical decision. Do not rank cohorts by interview count. Before selecting a segment, each cohort must have decision-quality evidence of an actual purchase/attempt, named workflow and budget/contracting authority, baseline and a transaction-specific alternative comparison; continue sampling any cohort without that evidence and probe counterexamples. Unknown capital or custody parties remain open readiness gaps. A supplier must currently sell programmatic services, describe real fulfillment and cash-flow risks, and identify integration and settlement decision-makers. Continue until evidence is decision-useful and split or reject cohorts when materially different workflows or economics emerge. Do not count multiple people from one organization as independent buyers or use a majority of a tiny sample as proof. These commercial discovery cohorts do not constitute the privacy anonymity set; PD-04 must separately assess the actual deployed independent borrower population and activity window.

|---|---|---|

Evidence for one trigger can validate a wedge, not the full product or the other capabilities. Preserve the complete destination and hard acceptance requirements. If no feature matters beyond existing controls, pivot or integrate; do not infer a privacy/credit need from general interest in agents |
| H1: Agent purchasing is a present operational problem in one or more cohorts | For each cohort, a recent agent purchase/attempt, current method, amount/frequency, technical/workflow owner, contracting/software budget owner and line item, manual steps, incident and cost | No interview vote threshold. Keep counterexamples visible |

Public-reader view includes chain-visible state, transaction linkage and publicly observable sender/payer/DUST/timing. These small samples guide product decisions; they do not establish market-wide prevalence or statistical certainty. Sequence the work: (1) buyer discovery establishes recent tasks and alternatives; (2) same-workload comparison measures whether Line adds a specific paid-for benefit; (3) prepare distinct buyer-funded control, independent-credit and supplier-credit experiments, each with its own payer/creditor and evidence; execute synthetic/test-asset arms first and admit real funded customer use only through the conjunctive entry gate; (4) test settlement separately with a named custodian and funded asset when direct buyer payment would bypass Line; (5) an external-credit pilot requires signed borrower, lender, supplier and settlement terms; a supplier-credit pilot requires signed debtor, supplier/creditor and settlement terms; both require verified asset movement/credit extension and legal/custody review; (6) paid adoption requires a completed pilot and received customer payment or equivalent binding order. Interest at one stage does not pass the next. Failure is actionable evidence, not a reason to relabel the same interviews as success.

### Interview protocol

1. Obtain consent for notes and any quotation. Record participant role, qualification, date and whether affiliation may be disclosed.
2. Ask about the last real agent purchase before describing Line. Capture who authorized it, paid it, fulfilled it, reconciled it and handled failure.
3. Ask which information was disclosed, to whom, and what harm followed. Avoid asking the leading question “Would you like private budgets?”
4. Identify whether the organization would trust a central gateway and why. Discuss the existing wallet/x402 alternatives fairly.
5. Show the complete proposed lifecycle, including public amount/history leakage where present, liability, reserves, fees, delivery, payout latency and recovery. Ask which step makes acceptance impossible.
6. Offer a specific pilot task, responsible integration owner and success criteria. Record objections and conditions verbatim in private notes; publish only consented, redacted findings.

Store interviews outside the public repository unless explicitly cleared for publication. Publish a redacted evidence summary that links to consented artifacts rather than publishing secrets, personal data or an organization's confidential budget.

## Pilot protocol and measurement

Before any real funded customer pilot, record a passed [conjunctive entry gate](PRODUCT_DIRECTION.md#entry-gate-for-a-real-funded-customer-pilot). Required full-history privacy, independent signing custody, buyer-accepted bound terms, tested proof/receipt/recovery paths, named accepted operating roles and legal/custody review are prerequisites together. Synthetic/test-asset research may continue without asserting those gates are passed; actual customer deposits and payment receipts are created and verified during the authorized pilot, not invented as prior evidence.

A fleet/multi-merchant functionality trial includes an accountable organization, at least three independently provisioned agents and two independently operated merchant identities. A full private-credit customer pilot additionally requires the number of independently accountable borrower organizations and activity window justified by the PD-04 threat model; three agents under one organization do not supply that anonymity cohort. The current one-borrower known-zero model cannot pass full book confidentiality. The core buyer-task trial must use the service sequence actually required by the observed task; if it needs only one supplier, test the second merchant with a separate purchase rather than inventing a dependency. A same-developer simulator rehearsal is useful engineering evidence and must be named a rehearsal. It does not validate merchant demand or issuer economics.

Choose the task and the minimum required service sequence from observed buyer research. If the task actually requires multiple steps, later services must consume or materially depend on earlier results; if independent purchases are more realistic, evaluate them as separate purchases rather than inventing a dependency. Give each agent issuer-approved purchasing authority. Do not call the allowance confidential until the design passes full-transcript tests, and do not call the pilot funded until the parties identify and show an actual asset flow. Provide authenticated quote terms, independent merchant keys, a delivery policy and a funded settlement path agreed by the pilot parties. Units must map to an identified asset and decimal scale; until that exists, label amounts “credit units.”

For an initial operational pilot, run at least 100 eligible purchase attempts and 40 adversarial/fault cases, stratified by merchant, agent, amount band, concurrency and financing arm; these are coverage floors for finding integration defects, not evidence of production-grade reliability or market demand. Include duplicate request, wrong merchant, modified terms, over-limit draw, reused receipt, expired note, disabled merchant, stale opening, crash after submission and payout confirmation timeout. If a buyer requires a reliability claim such as a maximum failure probability, pre-register the confidence level and tolerated failure rate, then size a separate trial accordingly; for example, zero failures in 100 independent trials still does not establish 99% reliability at 95% confidence. Report exact intervals and all exclusions.

| Measurement | Proposed gate | Required artifact |
|---|---|---|
| Correct money/debt/service reconciliation | Zero unmatched debits, repeated payouts, unearned repayment credits or duplicate paid deliveries | Per-purchase journal joining request, quote, note, redemption, asset receipt and delivery receipt; final signed reconciliation |
| Healthy-path operational checkpoint | At least 99/100 eligible attempts complete; every other attempt has a documented reconciled terminal or pending state. This is a pilot continuation rule, not a 99% reliability claim. | Unedited run log, sampling frame and confidence interval; report all failures, not only successful reruns |
| Unauthorized behavior | Zero acceptance of adversarial authorization/replay cases | Case inputs, expected outcome and actual contract/service state |
| Recovery | Every injected crash restores the same purchase/opening and resolves confirmation before new payment | Restart logs and pre/post state evidence |

| Merchant independence | B cannot redeem A's note; merchants do not possess issuer/agent secrets | Separate process/profile evidence and failed theft traces |
| Primary buyer value | Meet the budget-owner-signed total-cost threshold or the explicitly priced, pre-approved premium rule in H5 | Same workload, alternatives and complete cost ledger, with each input tagged measured, quoted or assumed |
| Operational friction | Compare manual interventions and reconciliation time. Any target improvement, including the former 30% aspiration, is a hypothesis to agree with the buyer before the trial, not a universal product gate. | Equivalent workload, intervention definition, numerator/denominator and operator feedback |

Run the baseline under comparable conditions; include x402 batch settlement when many small repeated calls are the relevant workload. Report network fees, proving resources, payout fees, capital costs, integration time and failures, not only a circuit execution duration.

## Economics and pricing experiment

Separate three accounts: issuer capital/risk, merchant receivables/payouts and Line software revenue. Capacity reserved in a contract does not pay a merchant or produce revenue. Repayment changes debt; capital replenishment must reconcile an actual asset movement. The sum of agent allowances is exposure authorization, not evidence of equal cash on hand.

Test willingness to pay separately on all three sides: (1) buyer software subscription or per-successful-checkout charge versus its current wallet/gateway/invoice cost; (2) issuer/capital compensation for committed capital, default exposure and collections; and (3) merchant price for the payout SLA/claim risk compared with current invoice terms or factoring. State the payer, billable event, failed/refunded-purchase treatment and whether lender compensation is distinct from Line revenue. A required fee must be authenticated and enforced; an agent-selected optional `fee` is not a reliable revenue mechanism.

For external credit, calculate funding expense from the actual quoted facility terms, asset, funded balance over time, undrawn commitment fees, tenor, day-count, compounding and rounding conventions. Under a fully prefunded model, the cost of the time-weighted funded reserve includes both used and unused liquidity; a drawn-principal calculation is an allocation or alternative comparison, not a second funding charge to add to it. Under a commitment model, combine only the independently contracted undrawn charge and drawn interest, with no balance counted twice. Show utilization and unused capacity required by the payout promise. Add expected default loss, custody, settlement, collections and liquidity/operating costs to the account of the party that actually bears them; show transfers between buyer, Line, creditor and merchant explicitly rather than charging the same expense to Line and the creditor twice. Compare with actual lending/factoring or supplier invoice terms. For buyer-funded control, use buyer treasury costs and do not invent a lender return. All rates, balances and loss assumptions need named counterparties' quotes or observed data, tagged measured, quoted or assumed.

For each pilot, compute Line software contribution as actual software revenue received minus the infrastructure, proving, settlement/payout, custody, support, integration, financing, default, refund and dispute costs contractually borne by Line. Separately compute creditor capital/loss economics and merchant net proceeds, assigning each expense or inter-party fee once to its responsible account; present a consolidated system-cost view that removes internal transfers. Freeze the measurement period and billable event; distinguish earned/invoiced software revenue from actual cash collected, and explicitly allocate subscriptions and integration costs rather than assigning a full annual payment to one task. Record each input as measured, quoted or assumed; show duration and utilization of any fully backed reserve. A contract counter is not evidence that the matching money exists or remains available. Do not combine an assumed lender fee with measured software revenue or report `feeReserve` as collected cash. Positive unit economics and buyer willingness remain unproven until those inputs are real.

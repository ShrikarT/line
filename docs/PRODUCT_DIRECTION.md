This is a decision about what Line is trying to become and what can currently be claimed; it does not reduce the destination to fit the competition deadline.

**Strategic bet to validate:** Line is privacy-preserving credit and checkout infrastructure for organizations whose production agents purchase programmatic services from independently operated suppliers. The organization is the accountable borrower; its agents receive bounded authority; capital providers fund the facility; merchants verify a claim and a separately named settlement operator pays it. The proposed advantage is that a merchant-specific purchase can be verified against shared organizational credit without giving an unauthorized observer the borrower's full credit book, while real fulfillment and asset settlement reconcile to the same purchase. That advantage exists only if the full-transcript privacy gate, independent custody and asset settlement all work. The customer is not selected. Compare (A) an agent-platform operator buying for its own production workflow, (B) an enterprise team operating agents for internal procurement, and (C) a platform integrating for a named customer that is the actual buyer/borrower. The user's initial enterprise-team hypothesis remains a live cohort, not a rejected alternative. Choose a lead segment only after a real purchase, job owner, software budget owner, liable borrower, existing control and contracting path are established in that segment. Line has not shown this problem is frequent, costly, or unsolved by delegated wallets, x402/MPP, a private gateway, cards, invoices or supplier terms; no market, privacy or cost superiority is established.

**One-sentence value hypothesis to test:** An organization can give its agents bounded purchasing access across independent API/compute suppliers using shared repayable credit, while each supplier verifies its own claim and outsiders cannot reconstruct the organization's wider credit book. The last clause is not true of the current implementation and must remain a hypothesis until the privacy gate passes.

**What the current build proves:** local generated-Compact execution can enforce a committed credit constraint, create a one-time merchant-bound claim, reject replay, and exercise fee, expiry, compensation, and recovery rules. It does not prove full-history confidentiality, independent role custody, cash backing, service delivery, or asset settlement. The current build is a protocol prototype, not an operating private-credit product.

**Destination:** organizations delegate bounded purchasing across fleets of agents and suppliers; independent capital providers take explicit, risk-priced exposure; suppliers receive the terms and amount required for their own purchases; unauthorized observers cannot reconstruct a borrower's or agent's credit book or attribute additional purchases to it; and every claim reconciles to authenticated fulfillment and actual asset settlement. Here “remaining credit capacity” means private borrower headroom `H = L - B`; it is distinct from public aggregate reserve available for claims, `T - E - R - F - P - U - X`, whose totals and deltas remain disclosed. Preserve public per-note principal/fee amounts and aggregate reserve totals/deltas as required disclosures. The current contract additionally publishes an agent pseudonym and linked identity/merchant/transaction history; these are current transcript facts, not automatically immutable product requirements, and a privacy redesign may need to remove per-agent identifiers and event links. A merchant and buyer are authorized to know their own purchase amount and clearance result: a successful draw for known principal `A` and fee `F` proves only that **pre-draw** borrower headroom satisfied `H >= A + F`; it does not reveal exact pre-draw or post-draw headroom. Do not promise general purchase-amount or counterparty secrecy while those data are public. Keep fleet operation, independent capital, the stated book-confidentiality requirement, risk/default management, portable credit history, full settlement and additional rails in scope. The current linked public history reconstructs debt from a known-zero opening. That is a direct failure of the required privacy outcome and a release blocker, not permission to weaken it. A target design must pass full-transcript inference tests before claiming the destination.

**Customer discovery, not a selected beachhead:** compare three cohorts separately: (A) agent-platform operators buying for their own production workflow; (B) enterprise teams operating agents for internal API/compute procurement; and (C) agent platforms integrating on behalf of a named customer who owns the purchase. Do not pool their results or designate one the lead ICP until the same evidence fields reveal a stronger job, buyer, liable party and route to adoption. Use a comparison memo, not an interview-vote score: compare evidence quality and independence, measured harm against the strongest viable alternative, named budget/contract authority, legal borrower and capital/custody path, buyer-valued requirement, procurement/security/vendor-onboarding and support burden, and a credible priced-pilot route. The initial target of two independent organizations per cohort is only a comparison floor; continue recruitment if any cohort lacks a real transaction or comparable evidence. A provisional lead can be named only when the same job recurs in at least two independent organizations in that cohort, both have an evidenced buyer/budget owner and plausible contracting/adoption path, and counterexamples and stronger rival-cohort evidence are documented. This is prioritization, not product-market-fit evidence. If these conditions do not distinguish a cohort, record **no segment wins; continue discovery**. For each real purchase or blocked attempt, record the task and trigger, date, amount/frequency, services needed, workflow owner, technical operator, software budget owner and budget line, legal buyer/borrower, authorizer, payer, supplier, capital source, reconciliation owner, current control, actual failure/harm and its measured baseline, why the strongest viable wallet/x402/MPP/gateway/card/invoice alternative failed, and the person who can sign a pilot. If the platform only integrates, include the customer buyer/borrower and establish who contracts with Line. Missing roles are unknowns, not filled assumptions. Treasury-only participants remain a separately labeled control cohort unless they operate the agent workflow. Segment high-volume low-value calls from larger API/compute invoices because batching, risk, sales path and settlement differ. For each cohort compare the smallest service sequence that completes an observed task; a second merchant is useful only when the actual task needs it or as a separate multi-merchant test. Independent parallel purchases are not a dependent workflow. The local demo's parallel deterministic services are scaffolding, not customer evidence.

## Entry gate for a real funded customer pilot

A real funded Line customer pilot requires all of the following for its chosen deployment and workload; milestone lettering or deadline pressure is not permission to bypass them:

- Independent issuer/agent/merchant key custody and the enrollment/prover boundary pass PD-02. The issuer must not hold the enrolled agent's signing secret; distinct UI roles are insufficient.
- The full PD-04 privacy criterion passes for the intended observer model, disclosure boundary and independently accountable borrower/activity cohort. The commercial ICP interview groups are not the privacy anonymity set: the actual deployed pool and activity window must independently satisfy PD-04. The current singleton known-zero history fails. A proof of a narrower predicate or a single organization's multi-agent test cannot substitute for required book confidentiality.
- Authenticated buyer-accepted service, request, denomination/scale, price/fee, deadlines, fulfillment, compensation/refund and dispute terms are bound to the quote/draw/note lifecycle (PD-01/08/09); accepted limits/default responsibilities and old-claim preservation are defined (PD-12).
- The exact source, compiled release/keys, configuration and migration policy are frozen, reproducible and bound to the tested deployment (PD-15/18). Supported proof/finality and receipt-verification paths have passed test-asset end-to-end/fault checks, and durable independent-role recovery/reconciliation is demonstrated (PD-03/05/06/07/10). Privacy must be assessed on that actual path, including payer, DUST, prover and timing metadata.
- Named borrower/creditor/capital source, merchants and custody/settlement operator accept written duties, asset and exposure limits, payment/refund/default/insolvency loss allocation and review requirements under PD-16. Initial release authority and safe migration/claim preservation under PD-18 are specified. A role list or unsigned terms do not pass.

Entry is assessed with test-asset evidence and signed operating terms; it does not presume receipts for a customer transaction that has not happened. During the pilot, authenticate each real inflow before allowing its backing to support a draw; bind every payout/repayment/refund to its exact obligation, and reconcile ambiguous confirmation before retry. Pilot exit needs actual funded deposit, payout, service, repayment and reserve-replenishment receipts and agreed economics. Do not feed live borrower books or production purchase authority into the current known-failing privacy/custody route. Synthetic rehearsals, consented private discovery and test-asset integrations can continue while the entry gate is open, with their limitations accurately labeled.

Keep commercial roles separate in research and contracts: the **user/operator** runs agents; the **buyer/product owner** chooses the workflow; the **budget owner** authorizes Line software spend; the **borrower** owes credit; the **capital provider or supplier-creditor** bears named financing exposure; the **merchant** fulfills and receives payment; and the **custodian/settlement operator** controls asset movement. One organization may occupy several roles, but record each role and conflict explicitly. In buyer-funded mode the buyer pays Line and the supplier; in external-credit mode the buyer pays Line, owes the lender, and the settlement operator pays the supplier; in supplier-credit mode the buyer owes the supplier/assignee, while the contract must identify who pays the supplier and when. Do not infer a payer from a technical role.

The organization must be the liable borrower unless a named alternative legal counterparty is documented. An agent is a delegated capability, not an assumed debtor. The initial buyer may also be its own issuer/capital source for a controlled pilot; that topology is a hypothesis, not evidence of third-party credit. Independent lending requires an identified lender, underwriting, exposure/default policy, repayment owner and legal/commercial review. Cryptographic authorization does not establish creditworthiness or liability.

Before calling the first pilot a credit product, document its cash-flow topology: liable borrower, capital source, asset and scale, custodian, settlement account, who pays each merchant, exact payout trigger/SLA, source/authentication of payout and repayment receipts, who bears non-delivery/chargeback/default loss, and how repayment cash replenishes the settlement pool. The current `fundReserve` circuit changes a counter only. Repayment restores private credit capacity; it does not replenish reserve accounting. The operator must separately reconcile an actual incoming asset and reserve funding. Do not count both a counter and an unobserved bank balance as capital.

## The purchase that proves the product

The local evaluation runs Merchant A's text analysis and Merchant B's processing estimate in parallel on the same input. These separate deterministic local HTTP services do not form a discovered customer workflow, do not consume one another's output, and are not independently operated suppliers or a funded integration. Treat this as plumbing rehearsal. Choose the actual service sequence from buyer discovery; include a dependency between services only when the observed task has one. Do not create artificial service coupling merely to make a demo look multi-step. Until discovery supplies it, the core job remains an unfilled evidence record: “When [trigger] occurs, [named buyer/user] uses [current workflow] to buy [service] for [purpose]; [specific failure/harm] costs [measured baseline]; [payer/borrower and budget owner] will adopt Line only if [pre-agreed improvement] holds against [best current alternative].”

1. The issuer establishes a line and settlement arrangement. Each role has an independent credential and private store. Authorized users see the configured environment, domain, credit unit, asset/currency and scale.
2. The destination quote must authenticate service, price, units/asset, request or invoice identity, expiry, fulfillment rule, and buyer-accepted refund/compensation policy, bound to merchant, instance and line generation. The current quote preimage binds merchant, amount, invoice ID, expiry, nonce, generation and issuer-approved fee schedule; it does not bind service description, denomination/scale, fulfillment rule or per-quote compensation/refund terms. The current buyer therefore has not cryptographically accepted those missing terms. Add required bindings to the existing quote/draw/note lifecycle within the twelve named circuits, compile them and reject altered terms; do not add a thirteenth circuit or assume unsupported Compact APIs.
3. The agent selects the needed service under issuer policy and obtains clearance. A confirmed generated-Compact draw reserves accounting capacity and creates the merchant note. That local result is not a network confirmation. Rejected draws show **"Clearance could not be proven."**
4. The note travels through a specified authenticated private transfer. The merchant verifies the exact finalized claim or, in the local evaluation, the generated-Compact result; durably records the invoice/note and idempotency key before fulfillment. The protocol must name which party authenticates the claim, how the merchant verifies the sender and exact terms, and what settlement guarantee the merchant accepts. Transporting a note opening is not itself a verified authorization.
5. The merchant or service operator returns a verifiable fulfillment receipt bound to the request, quote, claim and result reference. Its own durable idempotency record makes retries return the same logical result. Specify whether a receipt is signed, which key is authoritative, how the buyer verifies it and how corrections/disputes work; do not imply Compact proves off-chain service quality.
6. The authorized merchant redeems once. The application reconciles three distinct artifacts: the finalized claim receipt, the fulfillment receipt and the rail/custodian payment receipt bound to note, recipient, amount, asset and idempotency identity. Redemption does not imply payout. A receipt is trusted only to the extent of its named signer/verifier and finality/reversal policy; an issuer's unsupported report is not independent payment proof.
7. Treasury reconciles the obligation, actual repayment and reserve replenishment. Repeated receipts, webhooks or restarts cannot credit debt or pay an invoice twice. The agent has no repayment-acknowledgement tool.
8. Merchants and agents recover after reload, timeout, failed/ambiguous submission, crash and unavailable services. Non-delivery follows agreed compensation terms without double credit.

Product acceptance includes purchases at independently operated suppliers, cross-merchant theft rejection, repeated redemption rejection, declined clearance, an authentic repayment that restores capacity, failed fulfillment and agreed compensation, ambiguous-submission recovery, and actual payout linked to the invoice. Before a pilot, freeze the signed/authenticated term format: request hash, merchant/service, quote and claim identity, amount/fee, denomination, deadlines, compensation/refund policy, payout recipient, fulfillment receipt schema, payment receipt schema, idempotency key, signing authority, verification procedure and dispute/finality rules. Implement required bindings using only the twelve named circuits and supported Compact operations; keep external fulfillment/payment verification explicitly attributed to the authorized signer or rail verifier. Local generated execution supplies development evidence only; network finality, service fulfillment, and asset payment each require their own attributable receipts. A claim receipt alone does not prove the supplier was paid.

The market cannot yet be described as one proven "revolving credit" product. Compare these models with qualified buyers and suppliers before locking the commercial offer:

The strategic destination is **organization borrowing from independent capital**, with a named settlement operator paying independent suppliers; this is the product Line must ultimately prove. It is not yet selected as a launch topology because no counterparties or terms exist. Validate three distinct hypotheses against the same observed workload: (a) buyer-funded procurement control, where the buyer is both payer and policy owner; (b) external credit, where the organization is borrower, an independent lender is capital provider and the buyer pays Line software fees; and (c) supplier credit, where the supplier is creditor and chooses whether to extend terms, bear receivable risk or sell the receivable. For each, record who pays Line, who owes principal/fees, who holds assets, when a merchant is paid, and who bears default, non-delivery, reversal and insolvency loss. A buyer-funded test where the buyer pays merchants directly can validate only policy/control value; a separate funded escrow/custodian test is needed to validate Line-mediated claim and payout reconciliation. Neither validates third-party lending. Keep all three models in scope, but do not describe them as one interchangeable experiment: only external credit and supplier credit test independent financing. Select an operational pilot only after the buyer, actual creditor/capital source, supplier and settlement/custody operator agree in writing to liability, asset custody, repayment/default, claim evidence, payout timing, reversals and loss allocation. The following zero-fee example makes the current **accounting** semantics explicit; units are illustrative and this is not a funded flow. It assumes a named capital provider supplies 100 units to an identified custodian for a named organization that is legally liable as borrower; after the merchant is paid from custody, that borrower repays the capital provider into the same settlement account. No such parties, asset, account, rail or repayment receipt exist in the current build. Start with 100 units of actual cash in that account and `T=100`, `E=R=F=P=U=X=0`; open a 40-unit private line at `B=0`.

| Stage | Actual settlement cash | `T` | `E` | `R` | Private `B` | Free contract capacity |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Opening | 100 | 100 | 0 | 0 | 0 | 100 |
| Draw 25 | 100 | 100 | 25 | 0 | 25 | 75 |
| Merchant redeems; payout not yet made | 100 | 100 | 0 | 25 | 25 | 75 |
| External payout 25 actually completes | 75 | 100 | 0 | 25 | 25 | 75 |
| Genuine repayment 25 received; before issuer accounting | 100 | 100 | 0 | 25 | 25 | 75 |
| Issuer acknowledges repayment | 100 | 100 | 0 | 25 | 0 | 75 |
| Issuer mirrors verified inflow with `fundReserve(25)` | 100 | 125 | 0 | 25 | 0 | 100 |

This only reconciles under the stated assumption that the 25-unit external payout and incoming repayment both actually happened, and that the same incoming asset is mirrored exactly once. `redeemDraw` does not prove payout; `acknowledgeRepayment` does not change `T`; `fundReserve` changes a counter but authenticates no deposit. Without separately verified rail receipts, these counters cannot establish solvency or liquidity. The current code verifies neither rail event; this sequence is a pilot acceptance test, not a shipped capability.

`totalReserve` is cumulative inflow accounting, not a live bank/token balance. `encumberedReserve` tracks active claims; `redeemedReserve` tracks claims consumed and subtracts their backing from free capacity; fees and refund budgets also consume backing. Repayment changes private debt but not `totalReserve`, so actual repayment cash must be independently verified and separately mirrored through reserve funding before capacity becomes available again. This accounting can model recycling only if every real inflow/outflow is reconciled to an authenticated asset receipt and the same funds are not counted twice. The circuits do not observe those assets. A payout failure after redemption can leave the accounting conservatively unavailable while the cash remains in custody; a payout made without a matching claim can make the reserve insolvent. Preserve these distinctions and pass the worked flow above before claiming liquidity, revolving capital or settlement guarantees.

## Four distinct economic events

| Event | What it establishes | What it does not establish |
|---|---|---|
| Credit authorization | The authorized transition satisfies the credit constraint and policy for this state | Creditworthiness, delivery or availability of cash |
| Reserve accounting | Counters allow the claim under `T >= E + R + F + P + U + X` | Cash custody or token backing; increasing a counter is not a deposit |
| Claim redemption | The designated merchant consumed a valid note and accounting changed once | Service delivery or bank/token payout |
| Actual asset payout | The selected rail finalized payment to the designated recipient, with independently checked receipt | Automatic repayment of debt or proof of delivery |

Current reserve units are accounting units. The target must bind them to a denomination, currency/asset, scale and custody/settlement mechanism. Never mix dollars, token atomic units and credit units. Interfaces require bounded integer arithmetic.

For a facility, define `L` (limit), `B` (debt), `A` (principal), `F_draw` (agreed fee), `T` (total reserve accounting), `E` (active claims), `R` (redeemed claims in this accounting model), `F` (earned fee reserve), `P` (pending fees), `U` (refund budgets) and `X` (reported refund budgets). A draw currently enforces `B + A + F_draw <= L` and `A + F_draw <= T - E - R - F - P - U - X`. Credit repayment and reserve replenishment are distinct. `R` is a claim counter, not proof of external settlement.

Capital remains necessary. Central reserve custody may change allocation and overhead, but does not create liquidity or establish measured capital efficiency. A singleton line does not demonstrate fleet pooling. Quantify benefits against the same workload and risk constraints only after measurement.

Fees require issuer-approved terms accepted by the buyer and bound to the purchase. The current Compact contract now fixes a public flat-plus-rounded-basis-point policy at issuer opening, binds it into merchant quotes and rejects any different draw fee. Consoles disclose price, fee and added debt before authorization. This implements price enforcement, while independent buyer consent/audit, independent billing and collection remain open. Pending fees now become earned at merchant claim redemption; unredeemed-expiry compensation reverses debt credit or creates a private cash obligation. Earned accounting still does not prove delivery or collection. `feeReserve` is not collected revenue: accounting issuer-owned capacity and debt does not establish money received. [Pricing evidence](FEE_POLICY_2026-10-10.md).

For unredeemed cancellation or non-delivery, define who bears loss and whether principal/fees reverse. The current unredeemed-expiry policy holds full backing, authenticates one allocation against current debt, and commits any cash remainder privately. Old-generation refunds leave new books untouched. Issuer reports retain backing and do not verify cash. Redeemed-but-undelivered purchases and discretionary disputes remain implementation dependencies. Compensation must use supported changes within the authoritative twelve-circuit interface and authenticated idempotent reconciliation; do not invent additional Compact entry points.

## Privacy destination and present boundary

The privacy contract separates book confidentiality from facts a participant is authorized to learn about its own transaction. **Credit-book guarantee required by the protocol:** protect borrower headroom `H = L - B` (`L` is the limit; `B` is outstanding debt) and prevent unauthorized observers from distinguishing protected books using their complete accessible views, beyond explicitly declared leakage. This is distinct from public aggregate reserve availability `T - E - R - F - P - U - X`, whose totals and deltas remain public. Do not use an undefined ?bounded inference? threshold as a privacy pass condition; apply the observer-specific negligible-distinguishing-advantage criterion and disclosure boundary in [PRODUCT_VALIDATION.md](PRODUCT_VALIDATION.md#falsifiable-customer-hypotheses). Rejected draws use the generic copy **"Clearance could not be proven."** A merchant and buyer are authorized to know their own quote, purchase amount, clearance/claim result and fulfillment/payment status. For known principal `A` and fee `F`, successful clearance reveals only that pre-draw `H >= A + F`, not exact pre- or post-draw headroom. This is a transaction-specific disclosure, not purchase anonymity. **Required public disclosures:** note principal/fees and aggregate reserve totals/deltas. The current singleton implementation also publicly exposes merchant registry keys, the selected merchant at draw, a stable agent pseudonym, contract/domain and linked transaction history, as inventoried in [PRIVACY.md](PRIVACY.md). These additional fields are not automatically required forever: the target architecture must state which it preserves and which it removes or replaces. If public disclosures or practical side information make protected books distinguishable beyond authorized leakage, privacy fails under the current boundary; field omission or UI redaction is not a pass. Hiding a merchant?s own amount/clearance conflicts with serving that merchant and publishing note amounts. `identityCommit` currently reuses the same pseudonym across instances when the same agent secret is reused; `lineCommit` itself is domain-bound but does not remove this linkage.

The current direct-field implementation does **not** provide complete history confidentiality: public draws and fees from a known-zero opening reconstruct $B$ before the first private repayment; subsequent public outcomes/deltas still expose information. `L` and borrower headroom `H = L - B` are not direct fields either, but repeated clearance outcomes may distinguish or bound them. Public closure reveals zero debt. These are material limitations, not merely wording risks. The explorer must not print private books, but hiding fields from the UI cannot remove public transcript or history evidence. Midnight distinguishes private witnesses from public ledger/transcript values; assess both. [Compact transcript reference](https://docs.midnight.network/api-reference/compact-runtime/interfaces/CallProofData).

**Privacy release gate:** define the protected subject as the legal borrower organization and its credit book; protect per-agent balances too wherever they reveal that book. Specify a separate view and authorized information for issuer, borrower/agent, each merchant, unrelated merchants, public readers, relayers, prover and settlement operator. Include all chain-visible arguments/outputs, ledger reads, state diffs, note amounts/fees, reserve totals/deltas, merchant disclosures, nullifiers, sender/payer/DUST metadata, timing and outcome data, plus logs only when that observer can access them. Do not combine information across actors into one fictitious observer. A remote prover sees its witness inputs; current checkout keeps issuer and agent capabilities in one process. The target must prove client-side isolation or explicitly trust and name a prover, and specify its witness/log access and retention. Define two challenge books that differ only in protected borrower facts but have identical required public outputs and observer-authorized knowledge. Require a construction-level computational indistinguishability argument with stated assumptions/security parameter and negligible observer advantage as defined in [PRODUCT_VALIDATION.md](PRODUCT_VALIDATION.md#falsifiable-customer-hypotheses); the only exemptions are explicit authorized disclosures, such as a merchant's own purchase and its capacity lower bound. Include known-zero opening, repeats/failures, repayment, closure/reopening, compensation, side information and migration in attacks. Publish attack code, frozen priors and purchase sizes, false-positive/negative rates, posterior inference and residual leakage; finite attacks can falsify but cannot prove privacy. A borrower-cohort/activity threshold is a fail-closed operating condition, not a substitute for the cryptographic criterion: the named privacy/threat-model owner must justify a minimum number of independently accountable borrowers and a minimum transaction/activity window from the declared observer and auxiliary-information model before any privacy claim or production launch. Test the threshold at its low-activity boundary with linkage/inference attacks. If the model cannot justify a defensible threshold, or known counterparties, metadata or public disclosures distinguish books, keep the privacy-dependent product/release blocked; do not choose an arbitrary count to manufacture a pass. Preserve required public note amounts/fees, reserve totals/deltas and all twelve circuit roles/invariants. If they make the target infeasible, explicitly block the privacy-dependent release; never relax the claim. `openLine` still needs `agentSecret()` during an issuer-authenticated call, so independent issuer/agent custody is a separate hard gate. Public `identityCommit = agentId(agentSecret())` is not domain-bound and reusing an agent key links it across instances.

|---|---|---|---|

### Product privacy architecture to investigate

The current singleton model cannot hide a borrower's debt from a public observer while exposing every purchase amount and reserve delta: its public identity/line and known-zero opening link the amounts to that book. A **private multi-borrower credit pool is a feasibility question, not a solved design**. Any per-agent policy would need to sit under the legal borrower's confidential state, not an explorer filter or a set of agent leaves under one publicly attributable borrower. One possible construction to investigate is a borrower-scoped commitment tree for $L$, aggregate $B$, epoch and facility state, with optional agent leaves for delegated limits and spending state. A shared root or unlinked identity alone does not establish transcript privacy. The construction must hide borrower identity, account paths and per-agent identifiers in public inputs, outputs and ledger keys, while existing `openLine`/`draw`/repayment/compensation/closure/default circuits enroll or update authorized leaves against the current pool root. Every public note amount/fee and reserve delta must still reconcile to the exact hidden leaf transition and preserve aggregate reserve solvency. This might prevent public assignment of activity to one borrower only if the complete transcript remains unlinkable and enough independent borrowers participate.

The candidate must preserve required public note amounts/fees, aggregate reserve totals/deltas and exact accounting, but may need to remove or replace the current public agent pseudonym and per-agent event links; treat this as an explicit versioned privacy-design change. Inventory every singleton global (`identityCommit`, `lineCommit`, status, generation, expiry and fee policy) and decide whether it is public pool state or a borrower commitment; per-borrower enrollment, lifecycle and zero-baseline events must not reintroduce links. Inventory every other field and state whether it remains public. The design does not promise amount or merchant anonymity and retains each merchant's authorized knowledge of its own purchase. It must avoid linkable borrower and agent identifiers across line, note, refund and nullifier lifecycles while allowing each role to prove authority. The current `openLine` requires issuer authentication and an `agentSecret()` witness in one call; specify and implement a separate consent ceremony/artifact or another supported proof boundary, then prove it without putting both signing secrets in one prover/operator's custody. A public wallet/fee-payer, DUST inputs, event timing, success/failure outcomes or known merchant/customer relationship may relink an event. Inspect finalized transactions, SDK proof data, payer metadata and application/prover logs. Specify deployment/version migration and recovery for existing commitments. Define the minimum anonymity cohort in independent borrower organizations and an activity threshold; the privacy-dependent facility must not operate below it. More agents or leaves under one borrower do not increase that cohort. The unresolved feasibility work includes private enrollment and membership/update proofs, current-root atomic serialization, stale-root rejection and lost-update recovery; private per-facility status/repayment/expiry behavior; closure/default semantics that do not publish a zero-debt test; and full-transcript attribution across recovery and migration.

This is a design hypothesis, not a claim that Compact 0.31.1 provides a private account tree, anonymous enrollment, relay or batch guarantee. Midnight's [private-contract guide](https://docs.midnight.network/guides/build-a-private-smart-contract) and [witness guide](https://docs.midnight.network/guides/private-state-witnesses) establish private circuit inputs, public ledger state and commitments, but do not prove unlinkable borrower updates. Before calling the direction technically feasible, build an architecture-spike matrix mapping every required transition to one of the fixed twelve circuit names or to a precisely bounded off-chain signer/rail trust boundary. Compile enrollment plus draw/repayment with pinned compiler 0.31.1/runtime 0.16.0; prove separate issuer/agent custody. Test forged enrollment/path, stale-root rejection and concurrent draws against the latest root, over-limit draw, exact public amount-to-leaf accounting, aggregate reserve solvency, overflow/underflow, replay and recovery. Compare challenge books per observer using identical required public amounts/deltas and identical authorized information for that observer; include chain state, sender/payer/DUST, timing and only the application/prover logs accessible to that observer. Model cross-party log access only when the observer model explicitly grants collusion. Test at the minimum independent-borrower cohort and under low-activity/known-counterparty cases. A two-leaf or one-organization multi-agent test proves functionality only, not borrower privacy. If the pinned toolchain cannot implement the transitions within the twelve names, shared role secrets remain necessary, concurrent root updates can violate accounting, or metadata identifies a borrower's other activity, record the design as infeasible under current constraints and keep the privacy-dependent product/release blocked. Do not weaken the guarantee or conceal required amounts/deltas to call it fixed.

Even if per-agent events become unlinkable, low activity, known counterparties, a public transaction sender or timing may identify an agent; the named privacy/threat-model owner must set and test the low-activity boundary before operation, and the system must fail closed below it. Public total throughput and aggregate reserve changes remain disclosed under the current contract, so hiding aggregate utilization would require a separate design and an explicit change to the disclosure boundary.

Authorized treasury/risk reporting needs consented selective disclosure and data-minimization/retention rules. Invoice descriptions, service requests/results and note openings require secure off-chain transport. A remote proof service is a separate confidentiality boundary; call proving client-side only when the executed configuration establishes it.

## Differentiation and adoption

Agent wallets already configure spending limits that agents cannot change, and agent wallet tooling documents paid API requests using x402 and automatic USDC payment. A prior Midnight hackathon report describes Latch, an AI-assistant wallet that keeps per-transaction limits, total budget, allowed category and amount spent private. That is direct thematic overlap and means Line cannot claim novelty from private agent spending limits alone; this earlier event is a competitive signal, not the Wave 2 scoring rubric. Line's current differentiation hypothesis is organization-level shared credit from an accountable borrower/capital source across independent suppliers, coupled to verifiable claims and actual settlement, while preventing observers from reconstructing the wider book. The current public history fails that privacy requirement and no capital/settlement counterparties exist, so this remains one hypothesis among several for discovery to test. If a policy wallet or gateway meets the buyer's task without independent credit, Line should integrate it or pivot the segment. [Coinbase spending controls](https://docs.cdp.coinbase.com/agentic-wallet/mcp/mcp-tools/show-wallet-app), [paid-service flow](https://docs.cdp.coinbase.com/agentic-wallet/cli/skills/pay-for-service), [Midnight's prior hackathon examples](https://midnight.network/blog/celebrating-seven-winners-from-mlh-x-midnight-july-hack).

Claims, batching and deferred settlement are not inherently unique. x402 documents escrow-funded cumulative vouchers, serving after verification, and later claim/settlement, with durable merchant storage and client recovery. This establishes a relevant comparison baseline, not equivalent private credit. [x402 batch settlement](https://docs.x402.org/schemes/batch-settlement).

Also benchmark retry idempotency and authenticated terms/results against existing protocol extensions. x402 describes request fingerprints and payment IDs for retry handling, and signed offers/receipts. Line must demonstrate its private revolving-credit difference alongside these baseline reliability features. [Payment identifier](https://docs.x402.org/extensions/payment-identifier), [signed offers and receipts](https://docs.x402.org/extensions/offer-receipt).

These are comparison dimensions, not a comprehensive competitor survey. Evaluate prepaid APIs, policy-controlled wallets, a centralized private-budget gateway, organization billing/cards and invoicing. Record why a buyer needs independent private credit proofs over existing controls, including which data each alternative reveals to each party; do not assume merchant visibility of wallet allowances without evidence. Do not claim all cards publish limits or public wallets expose off-chain credit arrangements. If no observed buyer job requires an independent privacy-preserving verifier, treat that proposed differentiation as unsupported and the full credit product as commercially unvalidated. A simpler policy-control integration may be tested as a separate product hypothesis; do not present it as the full Line destination or remove the destination's privacy, financing, settlement and risk requirements. [PRODUCT_VALIDATION.md](PRODUCT_VALIDATION.md) contains the evidence-gathering and comparison plan.

The current differentiation hypotheses include private credit-book confidentiality, independently verifiable bounded authorization, access to shared/revolving capital, and supplier settlement/reconciliation.

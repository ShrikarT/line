## Settlement recommendation and its limits

Midnight supports shielded custom tokens; its shielded-token guide uses compiler 0.31.1. The guide also documents a current notification constraint: contract-mediated shielded delivery reliably reaches the caller, while sending to another user lacks generated coin ciphertext notification. This favors **merchant-pull redemption**, with the merchant submitting the redemption and receiving its own payment, rather than claiming an implemented issuer push payout. The receiving wallet must independently confirm the asset and the protocol must bind the recipient to merchant approval. `ownPublicKey()` alone is a prover-claimed value and is not an authorization gate. [Official shielded-token guide](https://docs.midnight.network/tokens/shielded-token).

Start the technical adapter with a clearly labeled test asset, issuer funding and organizational repayment liability. This is an intermediate verification lane, not a replacement for real capital, custody or the full private-credit destination. Do not label a custom test token USD or USDC, and do not infer a dollar peg, redemption right or actual financing. NIGHT/tNIGHT is unshielded and DUST is non-transferable fee resource, so neither supplies the proposed shielded stable-value settlement claim. [Midnight token overview](https://docs.midnight.network/tokens), [funding/test-token limits](https://docs.midnight.network/guides/acquire-tokens).

For production USD settlement, Circle lists native USDC on Base and does not list Midnight in that supported-chain table. A USDC/Base integration can be researched, but its public transfers and cross-system receipt/custody boundaries require an explicit privacy and reconciliation design. No bridge, native Midnight USDC, regulated custodian, funding partner or enforceable borrower agreement is inferred. [Circle supported chains](https://developers.circle.com/circle-mint/supported-chains-and-currencies).
